Method

100 fixed questions, 4 engines, once a month

Instrument, what is counted, noise floor, changelog

The index is a fixed panel of 100 questions put to the same AI engines once a month through their search-grounded APIs. Every answer and every cited URL is stored and published. Nothing on the site is typed in; every page is generated from the rows.

Panel v1

Template Who is the best {vertical} in {city}, Florida?, frozen 2026-07-02. 10 verticals (roofer, AC repair company, plumber, personal injury lawyer, dentist, chiropractor, real estate agent, landscaping company, pest control company, CPA) by 10 cities (DeLand, Daytona Beach, Deltona, Ormond Beach, Port Orange, New Smyrna Beach, Sanford, Lake Mary, Orlando, Winter Park). A prompt is never edited. If the panel changes it gets a new version number and the old one stays comparable with itself.

Instrument

DataForSEO ai_optimization/{engine}/llm_responses/live with web_search: true, United States, English, no location override, no logged-in state. The model is part of the instrument: a month asked with a different model is a different instrument and is compared to earlier months as directional only. Movers are reported only between months with the same instrument. "Charge" is the DataForSEO task charge recorded per call, the figure the collector caps on.

July 2026 was collected for EVOIX's Central Florida AI Citation Study on ChatGPT gpt-5.3-chat-latest and Perplexity sonar. From November 2026 the panel runs on four engines with pinned models (ChatGPT gpt-5.4-2026-03-05, Perplexity sonar-pro, Claude claude-sonnet-5, Gemini gemini-3.6-flash), so the ChatGPT and Perplexity lanes changed instrument between month zero and month one. The model shown on every page is the one the API reported serving, not the one requested: in November 2026 the collector asked for gemini-3.5-flash and DataForSEO served gemini-3.6-flash on all 100 rows, so the Gemini lane is pinned to the served name from December.

Gemini returns every citation as a vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com redirect link. The collector follows each link once and stores the real URL; the redirect link is kept in the row as redirect_url. The handful that fail to resolve fall back to the domain in the citation title and are flagged in the row.

What is counted

  • Answers returned. A question is left without an answer when every attempt at it errored or returned no text. Those are counted and shown, and excluded from every rate, so "88 of 100" means 88 questions came back answered. An attempt that errored and was then answered on a retry is not a failed question; the number of such retries is shown per month above.
  • Answers with an extracted name. An answer counts if at least one business name is extracted from its bold text, its numbered headings or its table body rows (table headers are skipped). Since analysis a5 the extractor drops advice headings ("Get Multiple Estimates"), the model's own working headings ("Searching for recommendations"), ratings, addresses and phone lines, splits a bold run of three or more names into separate names, and treats a phrase made only of ordinary English words as a heading unless it carries a trade word (Roofing, Dental, Law, CPA and the like) or matches one of the answer's cited domains. Since a6 it also drops licence types and numbers, awards, certifications and accreditations, review-site phrases ("Google Reviews", "A+ BBB rating"), directory and review-site brands written as names ("Realtor.com", "Super Lawyers"), phrases made only of directory names and review words, regulators, associations and the bar (a department, institute, board, bureau or association), manufacturer contractor programmes ("Master Elite"), "X vs. Y" headings, check-list headings that start with an imperative or an adverb of advice (Verify, Confirm, Compare, Always, Never and a list of about twenty-five such words in scripts/analysis.py), single label words ("Doctors", "Referrals"), and the panel's own city names. The extraction is automatic and conservative. It misses a name written in plain prose, so "no extracted name" is not the same as "named nobody", and a phrase an engine wrote in bold can still slip through as a name when it is none of the above; those are filtered as they are found and logged below. The count on every page is therefore "answers with an extracted name", not "answers naming a business".
  • One business, several spellings. On a question's page the names from all engines are grouped into one listing when they differ only in case, punctuation, a parenthesised tail or an entity suffix (Inc, LLC, P.A.). "Hartsock & Mann, CPA" and "Hartsock & Mann CPA" are one business; "Dr. John Cervenka" and "John Cervenka" are kept apart. The spelling shown is the most frequent one.
  • Source classes. Each cited domain is one of three things in a given month. Directory: on the published list in scripts/classify.py (directories, aggregators, review, social, reference and lead-gen sites). Multi-city site: not on the list, but cited for the same template across 5 or more of the 10 cities that month, which catches regional businesses, franchises and business sites that publish "best in {city}" listicles. Own site: everything else, read as a local business's own website. The class is decided per month across all engines, so a domain has one class per month and can change class between months; the domain pages show the class for every month. Disclosure: delandlocal.com is a DeLand business directory published by EVOIX, the publisher of this index. It is on the directory list, so a citation of it never counts as a business's own site, and it appears in the tables like any other directory.
  • Own-site share. Each answer contributes its distinct cited domains once. The share is own-site citations divided by all domain citations (own site, directory and multi-city together), summed over the engine's answers that month. It is not the share of the engine's distinct domains across the whole month, which runs higher because directories repeat across answers and business sites mostly do not.
  • Most cited source. The number of answers citing a domain at least once; a domain counts once per answer.
  • Samples. One answer per question per engine per month. AI engines are not deterministic, so any single question's monthly result is provisional. The aggregate figures across 100 questions are the index. The noise floor is measured, not assumed, with the subsample below.

Same-day repeat subsample

With each monthly run, 10 of the 100 questions (one per vertical, city rotating) are asked three times on the same day on every engine. The agreement rate on the named businesses and on the cited domains is published here and is the floor under which a month-over-month move is noise. "Same first-named business" is an exact match on the first extracted name, so a spelling or suffix difference counts as a miss, and a group in which any sample extracted no name counts as disagreement. The name rates are therefore conservative, and a low figure on an engine that often names nobody (Claude in November 2026) mixes real instability with answers that declined to name a business.

Changelog

  • 2026-07-02: month zero collected (two engines) for the Central Florida AI Citation Study. Its published analysis (a1) split domains into two classes, aggregator and own site, with a 4-city rule; the figures on this site are recomputed under the current analysis and can differ from the study page.
  • 2026-11-02: Local AI Sources filed as EVOIX aeo-lab experiment EXP-015 with predictions written before the first monthly run. Domain registered. Analysis a3: nine domains appended to the July directory list (experience.com, lawnstarter.com, homeguide.com, trustanalytica.com, bing.com, apple.com, tiktok.com, x.com, twitter.com); the city rule became its own class, multi-city site, at 5 cities, decided per month across all engines, after a code review found the 4-city rule labelling regional businesses as directories; table header rows excluded from name extraction; failed calls counted and shown.
  • 2026-11-02: month one collected, four engines, 400 answers, 04:52 to 05:30 EST, charge $13.36. Analysis a4: 46 domains appended to the directory list after reading the November top sources on the two new lanes and the long tail (directories, aggregators, ranking and listicle sites, media, reference, government, professional associations, manufacturer and insurer finders; the full list is in scripts/classify.py, including delandlocal.com, disclosed above). Nine domains cited in three or more answers could not be verified today and are left to the multi-city rule for now: smarfle.com, theroofingbrief.com, legaldirectorate.com, toptaxhq.com, prosgrade.com, cityvetted.com, myheronhome.com, roof.info, hvacservice.io. Gemini redirect links resolved to real URLs (above). The collector stops on a confirmed empty account balance and treats a one-off 40200 as a retryable row.
  • 2026-11-02, later the same day: analysis a5. A review of the first November build found about one extracted name in ten was not a business (an address, a star rating, a line of advice such as "Verify Licensing & Insurance", or three names bundled in one bold run; about one in five on the Gemini lane). The extractor now filters those as described under "Answers with an extracted name"; the word list it uses is published as data/generic-words.txt. Naming rates moved with it (Claude 76 to 69 of 100, Gemini 100 to 99). July is recomputed under the same rule.
  • 2026-11-02, evening: analysis a6, with the redesign. Two independent checks of the rebuilt pages found strings listed as businesses that were none: a licence type ("Certified Roofing Contractor (CCC)" on the DeLand roofer page), bare directory and review-site names (Yelp, Zillow, Justia, Super Lawyers), regulators and associations (the Florida DBPR, the FICPA, the Florida Bar), label words ("Doctors", "Referrals"), city names and check-list headings. The filters named under "Answers with an extracted name" drop them: 160 of 2,116 extracted names in November (about one in 13) and 71 of 1,303 in July (one in 18). Numbered headings ("### 1. Name", including "Top for X: Name") are now read as list items. Each question's page groups the businesses by how many engines named them and merges spellings as described under "One business, several spellings". Rates moved with it: Claude 69 to 64 of 100 (five answers whose only extracted strings were a regulator, a directory or a label now count as none), Gemini 99 to 100 (a numbered-heading answer now counts), ChatGPT and Perplexity unchanged at 100 and 100; July unchanged at 94 and 100. The same check found that "named a business" overstated what the count measures, so every such label now reads "answers with an extracted name". Dates on the site are the collection day in Florida time (previously the UTC day; no date changed).

Related work

Three public indexes measure AI citations at the global, category level and are worth reading alongside this one: Promvia's AI Source Index (weekly, 40 buyer questions, every row published), BrandInsightAI's AI Source Bias Index and HeyAmos's AI Sources Index. This index is the local complement: fixed cities, fixed verticals, named businesses, monthly, from July 2026.

Corrections

If a name was extracted wrongly, a domain is misclassified, or a business believes an engine's answer about it is inaccurate, write through the EVOIX contact page. Extraction and classification errors are fixed in the next build and logged above. Engine answers themselves are reproduced as returned and are not edited.
